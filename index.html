Inside the Apple Home Collection: Why Apple Let LG Build Its Smart Home Hardware

A teardown of Apple's dual-pronged smart home architecture—pairing a first-party Liquid Retina command hub with co-engineered residential hardware built by LG Electronics and Schneider Electric.

1. The Two-Pronged Hardware Strategy

Rather than manufacturing low-margin, high-liability residential fixtures in-house, Apple split its smart home ecosystem at the drywall boundary. Apple retains complete ownership of the silicon and display layer—the HomeView 7-inch smart display, Apple TV 4K, and HomePod mini—while partnering with LG Electronics and Schneider Electric to manufacture the physical sensors, actuators, and high-voltage wall hardware.

2. What Makes the Engineering Unique

  • UWB + Aliro Spatial Unlocking: Standard smart locks rely on 2.4 GHz Bluetooth RSSI signal strength, which struggles with multipath reflections. By fusing Bluetooth handshakes with Ultra-Wideband Time-of-Flight (ToF) ranging and angle-of-arrival (AoA), the deadbolt resolves exterior vs. interior approach vectors within ±5 cm.
  • Hardware-Enforced Privacy Kinematics: Instead of relying on a software LED indicator or a thin plastic slider, the indoor camera pitches its optical barrel 95° downward into an enclosed aluminum cup via a micro-stepping actuator, physically blocking the CMOS sensor while breaking the microphone circuit through a leaf switch.
  • 60 GHz Radar & Local Edge Compute: The thermostat embeds a 60 GHz millimeter-wave radar sensor behind its cover glass to detect micro-movements and room occupancy without an optical camera.
