## 1. Executive Hardware Teardown

In high-pressure continuous-flow thermoblocks, heat transfer efficiency is governed by the boundary layer laminar sublayer rather than raw heater wattage. When cold fluid is delivered at high pressures across tight capillary conduits, stagnant fluid coats the conduit walls while core fluid streams through unheated.

## 2. Hydrodynamic Boundary Layer Dynamics

Fluid passing through the capillary conduit develops a parabolic velocity profile governed by the Navier-Stokes momentum equations. Because fluid velocity at the tube wall approaches zero (no-slip condition), heat transfer from the 98°C AISI 304 wall into the core fluid is constrained by molecular thermal conduction across the stagnant viscous boundary layer.

## 3. Manufacturing & Thermal Bonding Sequence

To prevent thermal contact resistance ($R_{th}$) spikes at the stainless-steel-to-aluminum interface, casting and mechanical swaging follow strict progressive parameters:

<ProcessFlow>
  <ProcessStep step="01" title="Rotary-Draw Capillary Bending" parameters="AISI 304 • OD 4.0mm / ID 2.4mm">
    Annealed stainless steel tube is bent on a CNC rotary-draw mandrel with pressurized internal oil fill to limit cross-sectional ovality under 3.5%.
  </ProcessStep>
  <ProcessStep step="02" title="Pre-Formed Cavity Pre-Heating" parameters="280°C pre-soak">
    Stainless capillary conduit and high-output tubular calrod elements are seated into hardened H13 tool steel dies to suppress chill-pin formation.
  </ProcessStep>
  <ProcessStep step="03" title="High-Pressure Squeeze Casting" parameters="A380 Alloy • 65 MPa dwell">
    Semi-solid liquid A380 aluminum alloy is injected under high pressure to metallurgically bed the conduit, eliminating insulating interfacial air micro-voids.
  </ProcessStep>
  <ProcessStep step="04" title="Hydrostatic Calibration Burst Test" parameters="18 Bar • 30s dwell">
    The finished assembly is pressurized with deionized water to gauge capillary bore retention and confirm zero leakage along the swaged interface.
  </ProcessStep>
</ProcessFlow>
